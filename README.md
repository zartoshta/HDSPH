# HDSPH
Higher-Dimensional Signal Projection Hypothesis (HDSPH)
The Higher‑Dimensional Signal Projection Hypothesis (HDSPH) frames transient astrophysical signals as noisy, operator‑induced projections of a latent stochastic field rather than direct emissions from uniquely identifiable sources. HDSPH replaces metaphysical appeals to extra spatial dimensions with a mathematically precise inverse‑problem formulation: observations follow S_obs = H[M] + η, where M is a latent random field, H a measurement operator, and η observational noise. The framework quantifies recoverable information via mutual information, models events as point processes driven by a latent intensity, and predicts operator‑dependent signatures such as convolutional distortion, event fragmentation, heavy‑tailed fluence distributions, and cross‑instrument anisotropies. HDSPH is explicitly falsifiable: it yields concrete statistical tests (overdispersion, tail‑index estimation, mutual‑information bounds, cross‑correlation and entropy anomalies) and prescribes observational programs to discriminate projection‑induced explanations from conventional emission models. The hypothesis is retained only when it demonstrably improves predictive performance or interpretability relative to simpler null models; otherwise, it reduces to a descriptive reparameterization of known phenomena.
